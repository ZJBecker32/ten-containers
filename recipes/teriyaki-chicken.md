## Teriyaki Chicken

**Serves:** 10
**Prep Time:** 10 min
**Cook Time:** 4–5 hrs

**Ingredients:**

  - ~5 ¾ lbs chicken breast, whole (not cubed) — package weight; trim before cooking
  - ½ cup chicken broth
  - 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice (dry)
  - 4 bags frozen broccoli florets (~1360 g total)
  - ⅔ cup low-sodium soy sauce (load-bearing — see Notes)
  - ¾ cup low-sodium chicken broth
  - 8–9 cloves garlic, minced
  - 2 tbsp fresh ginger, minced (or 2 tsp ground)
  - 3 tbsp brown sugar
  - 2 tbsp rice vinegar (or cider vinegar)
  - 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  - 2 tbsp cornstarch + 4 tbsp cold water
  - Salt, pepper
  - Scallions, sesame seeds, sriracha (added fresh when eating)

**Steps:**

1.  Place ~5 ¾ lbs whole chicken breasts in the crockpot, trimming any tough or unappealing parts first. Season with salt and pepper and add ½ cup chicken broth. No cutting required.

2.  Cook on High 4–5 hours, or Low 7–8 hours. Do not open the lid before hour 4 — each peek adds 15–20 minutes.

3.  Test for shreddability rather than for temperature. Chicken is food-safe at 165°F but will not pull apart until roughly 195–205°F, when the connective tissue breaks down. Pull a piece and try it with two forks; if it holds together in a chunk, give it another 30 minutes.

4.  Drain the cooking liquid completely. Unlike the buffalo recipe, none of it is reserved — this sauce is built to its own volume and thinning it with crockpot liquid is what makes teriyaki taste washed out.

5.  Shred the chicken with a hand mixer directly in the crockpot — about 30 seconds. A few pulses only; over-mixing turns it mushy.

6.  Weigh the shredded chicken now, before any sauce goes near it. This is the only moment a clean number exists — once the sauce is stirred in the two cannot be separated, and a sauced weight is not a yield.

7.  In a saucepan, combine ⅔ cup low-sodium soy sauce, ¾ cup low-sodium chicken broth, the minced garlic and ginger, 3 tbsp brown sugar, and 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil. Simmer 4–5 minutes to cook the raw edge off the garlic.

8.  Taste the sauce before thickening. Teriyaki should read savory first with the sweetness behind it. If it tastes like a dessert glaze, it has gone the wrong direction — pull back the sugar next time rather than adding salt to compensate.

9.  Whisk 2 tbsp cornstarch into 4 tbsp cold water, then stir the slurry into the simmering sauce. Cook 1–2 minutes until it coats the back of a spoon. This step is what lets the sauce ride in the container instead of pooling into the rice.

10. Remove the sauce from the heat and stir in 2 tbsp rice vinegar. The acid goes in last — simmering it dulls the brightness that keeps this from tasting flat.

11. Pour the sauce over the shredded chicken and stir. Hold on Warm 10–15 minutes so it absorbs.

12. During the last ~20 minutes of chicken time, rinse 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear. Add to the Instant Pot with 3 ¼ cups water (1:1). Measure the water carefully — an over-filled pot gives waterlogged rice that packs soggy.

13. Confirm the valve is set to SEALING. Pressure Cook (Manual) on High for 3 minutes, natural release 10 minutes, then quick release. Fluff and leave the lid off to cool.

14. Microwave the frozen broccoli in its bags per the package. It is already blanched, so it needs steaming rather than cooking. Tip it into a colander and shake off the water.

15. Cool the chicken, rice, and broccoli 10–15 minutes before portioning. Sealing hot food causes condensation and sogginess.

16. Assembly line across 10 open containers: 190 g sauced chicken, then 147 g rice, then 125 g broccoli. That 190 g is roughly 145 g of meat plus its share of sauce. No separate sauce container — the cornstarch is what makes that safe.

17. Label with a dry-erase marker. 6 containers to the fridge, 4 to the freezer. Move freezer containers to the fridge the night before eating.

**Notes:**

STILL UNTESTED. v5 changes three things at once, so every number here is
planned rather than observed. Weigh the shredded chicken (step 6), the cooked
rice, and the steamed broccoli, then promote with a real date.

**The bottled sauce is gone, and with it the reason this recipe never got
cooked.** Two bottles put it at ~1400 mg of sodium and 12 g of added sugar —
nearly double the ceiling and the worst numbers in the rotation. The scratch
sauce is the same soy-and-aromatics build that `ground-beef-broccoli` has
already proven at ~750 mg, with chicken broth in place of beef and a third
tablespoon of brown sugar, because teriyaki wants more sweetness than beef and
broccoli does. Projects at ~720 mg and 4 g of added sugar.

That projection carries the same warning the beef recipe carries:
**low-sodium soy is load-bearing.** ⅔ cup of regular soy would land this near
975 mg, over the ceiling. If Aldi only has regular, use ½ cup and add ¼ cup
more broth to hold the sauce volume. Do not swap one for one.

**Frozen broccoli replaces 5 ½ lbs of fresh florets**, which is where most of
the old 40-minute active time went: washing, cutting, oiling, two 12–15 minute
roasting batches, and a sheet pan to scrub. Buffalo measured frozen florets at
0.931 against 0.38–0.48 for fresh crowns, so it is both far less work and far
more predictable. 4 bags rather than buffalo's 3, because this recipe has
always run a heavier vegetable portion.

**The sauce is no longer packed separately.** v4 used ten small side
containers because bottled teriyaki is thin and would soak the rice. A
cornstarch-thickened sauce clings to shredded chicken instead of running off
it — the same reason the beef recipe packs no sauce either. That removes ten
containers to fill, label and wash, and it retires the open question in v4
about whether 42 g or 50 g per bowl was right. The question no longer exists.

Together those three changes take active time from 40 minutes to about 20,
which would make this the second low-effort recipe in the rotation. Buffalo is
currently the only one, and the next easiest cookable recipe is 45 minutes.

`per_container.chicken` is **sauced** weight, following buffalo. The 5 ¾ lbs is
planned against the 0.57 crockpot yield now in standing parameters, giving
~1450 g of meat plus ~450 g of sauce.

Note that `ground-beef-broccoli` counts its protein *unsauced* — 185 g of beef
with the sauce uncounted on top. The two recipes genuinely disagree about what
`per_container` means. Buffalo's convention is the one to keep, because it is
the number you can actually put on a scale, and the beef recipe should be
reconciled to it on its next cook rather than guessed at now.

At ~520 kcal this would be the lightest bowl in the rotation, which is a
consequence of lean shredded breast and a sauce that is mostly soy and broth
rather than oil. If it eats light, more rice is the cheap fix.

Reheats in a microwave, which is the only thing available at work. Rice and
chicken freeze well; the broccoli softens slightly but holds.
