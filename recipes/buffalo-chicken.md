## Buffalo Chicken Bowls

**Serves:** 10
**Prep Time:** 10 min
**Cook Time:** 4–5 hrs

**Ingredients:**

  - ~5 ¾ lbs chicken breast, whole (not cubed) — package weight; trim before cooking
  - ½ cup chicken broth
  - ⅔ cup Frank's RedHot
  - 6 tbsp butter
  - 2 tbsp white vinegar
  - ½ tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
  - 1 tsp garlic powder
  - 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice (dry)
  - 3 bags frozen broccoli florets (~1000 g total)
  - Salt, pepper
  - Ranch or blue cheese dressing, diced celery (added fresh when eating)

**Steps:**

1.  Place ~5 ¾ lbs whole chicken breasts in the crockpot, trimming any tough or unappealing parts first. Season with salt and pepper and add ½ cup chicken broth. No cutting required.

2.  Cook on High 4–5 hours, or Low 7–8 hours. Do not open the lid to check before hour 4 — each peek adds 15–20 minutes.

3.  Test for shreddability rather than for temperature. Chicken is food-safe at 165°F but will not pull apart until roughly 195–205°F, when the connective tissue breaks down. Pull a piece and try it with two forks; if it holds together in a chunk, give it another 30 minutes.

4.  Drain the cooking liquid, reserving ¾ cup. Draining first is what keeps the sauce from being watered down.

5.  Shred the chicken with a hand mixer directly in the crockpot — about 30 seconds. A few pulses only; over-mixing turns it mushy.

6.  Weigh the shredded chicken now, before any sauce goes near it. This is the only moment a clean number exists — once the sauce is stirred in the two cannot be separated, and a sauced weight is not a yield.

7.  Melt 6 tbsp butter, then whisk in ⅔ cup Frank's RedHot, 1 tsp garlic powder, the cayenne, and the ¾ cup reserved liquid.

8.  Remove the sauce from the heat and stir in 2 tbsp white vinegar. The acid goes in last, and here it is doing a specific job — see Notes.

9.  Pour the sauce over the shredded chicken and stir. Hold on Warm 10–15 minutes so it absorbs. Taste; add butter if it is too hot, a splash more vinegar if it reads flat.

10. During the last ~20 minutes, rinse 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear. Add to the Instant Pot with 3 ¼ cups water (1:1). Measure the water carefully — an over-filled pot gives waterlogged rice that packs soggy.

11. Confirm the valve is set to SEALING. Pressure Cook (Manual) on High for 3 minutes, natural release 10 minutes, then quick release. Fluff and leave the lid off to cool.

12. Microwave the frozen broccoli in its bags per the package. It is already blanched, so it needs steaming rather than cooking. Tip it into a colander and shake off the water.

13. Cool the chicken, rice, and broccoli 10–15 minutes before portioning. Sealing hot food causes condensation and sogginess.

14. Assembly line across 10 open containers: 190 g sauced chicken, then 148 g rice, then 100 g broccoli. That 190 g is roughly 145 g of meat plus its share of sauce — you weigh it sauced, because once it is stirred in you cannot separate them. No separate sauce container.

15. Label with a dry-erase marker. 6 containers to the fridge, 4 to the freezer. Move freezer containers to the fridge the night before eating.

**Notes:**

COOKED 2026-09-13, still untested. Rice and broccoli were measured cleanly;
the chicken was not, so nothing goes in `yields` and the status stays where it
is until one clean chicken number exists.

  rice       3 ½ cups dry -> 1580 g   (451 g per dry cup)
  broccoli   1020 g frozen ->  950 g   (yield 0.931)
  chicken    5.7 lbs raw  -> 1914 g   WITH SAUCE — not usable

Two things spoiled the chicken figure, and both were the recipe's fault. It
was weighed after saucing, and nothing in v1 said to weigh before. And the
5.7 lbs was package weight taken before trimming, so it counts meat that never
entered the pot. Backing the sauce out by ingredient gives an apparent 0.565,
but that is an estimate subtracted from an estimate and it sits below every
figure on file. Step 6 now exists so there is a moment where a clean number
can be taken.

Portioned 10 × 191 g sauced chicken, 158 g rice, 95 g broccoli — about 570
kcal and 51 g of protein, both in band.

This is built for a week with no time in it. Nothing touches an oven, there
are no sheet pans to wash, and the only knife work is dicing celery at eating
time. The crockpot runs unattended for four to five hours.

**Frozen broccoli is the deliberate choice, not a compromise.** Fresh crowns
would mean washing, trimming, cutting, oiling, two roasting batches and a
drain — 20–25 minutes, which is more than this recipe's entire hands-on
budget. Roasted tastes better; on a week like this it is not worth the time.

It also turns out to be far more predictable. Measured at 0.931 — 1020 g of
frozen florets gave 950 g steamed — against 0.38–0.48 for fresh crowns. You
can plan a frozen bag almost exactly; a crown carries an unknowable amount of
stalk. That is a second argument for frozen beyond the labour, and it applies
to any recipe here that wants a predictable vegetable weight.

**The sauce is cut to two-thirds and the tang rebuilt with vinegar.** Frank's
RedHot is 190 mg of sodium per teaspoon, so the 1 ½ cups a full-strength
version calls for lands around 1500 mg per container — worse than the bottled
teriyaki, and the worst number this rotation would have. ⅔ cup comes in near
760 mg, under the ceiling.

Cutting hot sauce alone would leave it bland, so the 2 tbsp of white vinegar
restores the acidity and the cayenne restores the heat, neither of which
carries any sodium. Same move as the ground beef sauce: rebuild the flavour
from unsalted components rather than accepting what the bottle gives you.

If you want the full-heat version, use 1 ½ cups Frank's and drop the vinegar
and cayenne. It will be better buffalo and roughly 1500 mg of sodium, and the
ranch adds another ~260 mg on top at eating time.

**Chicken is 5 ¾ lbs of package weight, not the 6 ½ an earlier draft
suggested.** Breast runs about 31 g of protein per 100 g against 26 for beef
and turkey, so it needs a smaller portion for the same protein. Planned
against the 0.65 crockpot yield in standing parameters, not the 0.76 that
draft assumed — 0.76 is the top of the observed range and the one clean
measurement this repo has is 0.637. v2 raised it from 5 ¼ because the first
cook showed trim losses that package weight does not capture.

`per_container.chicken` is **sauced** weight, ~190 g, of which roughly 145 g
is meat. That is what you can actually put on a scale: the sauce is stirred
through and the two do not separate. v1 said 155 g meaning meat alone, which
was not a number anyone could portion to.

Ranch goes on at eating time, never packed — it separates when reheated.
Celery diced fresh, not on prep day.
