## Buffalo Chicken Bowls

**Serves:** 10
**Prep Time:** 10 min
**Cook Time:** 4–5 hrs

**Ingredients:**

  - ~5 ¼ lbs chicken breast, whole (not cubed)
  - ½ cup chicken broth
  - ⅔ cup Frank's RedHot
  - 6 tbsp butter
  - 2 tbsp white vinegar
  - ½ tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
  - 1 tsp garlic powder
  - 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice (dry)
  - 3 bags frozen broccoli florets (~1000 g total)
  - Salt, pepper
  - Ranch or blue cheese dressing, diced celery (added fresh when eating)

**Steps:**

1.  Place ~5 ¼ lbs whole chicken breasts in the crockpot. Season with salt and pepper and add ½ cup chicken broth. No cutting required.

2.  Cook on High 4–5 hours, or Low 7–8 hours. Do not open the lid to check before hour 4 — each peek adds 15–20 minutes.

3.  Test for shreddability rather than for temperature. Chicken is food-safe at 165°F but will not pull apart until roughly 195–205°F, when the connective tissue breaks down. Pull a piece and try it with two forks; if it holds together in a chunk, give it another 30 minutes.

4.  Drain the cooking liquid, reserving ¾ cup. Draining first is what keeps the sauce from being watered down.

5.  Shred the chicken with a hand mixer directly in the crockpot — about 30 seconds. A few pulses only; over-mixing turns it mushy.

6.  Melt 6 tbsp butter, then whisk in ⅔ cup Frank's RedHot, 1 tsp garlic powder, the cayenne, and the ¾ cup reserved liquid.

7.  Remove the sauce from the heat and stir in 2 tbsp white vinegar. The acid goes in last, and here it is doing a specific job — see Notes.

8.  Pour the sauce over the shredded chicken and stir. Hold on Warm 10–15 minutes so it absorbs. Taste; add butter if it is too hot, a splash more vinegar if it reads flat.

9.  During the last ~20 minutes, rinse 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear. Add to the Instant Pot with 3 ¼ cups water (1:1). Measure the water carefully — an over-filled pot gives waterlogged rice that packs soggy.

10. Confirm the valve is set to SEALING. Pressure Cook (Manual) on High for 3 minutes, natural release 10 minutes, then quick release. Fluff and leave the lid off to cool.

11. Microwave the frozen broccoli in its bags per the package. It is already blanched, so it needs steaming rather than cooking. Tip it into a colander and shake off the water.

12. Cool the chicken, rice, and broccoli 10–15 minutes before portioning. Sealing hot food causes condensation and sogginess.

13. Assembly line across 10 open containers: 155 g chicken, then 148 g rice, then 100 g broccoli. No separate sauce container — the buffalo sauce is already in the chicken.

14. Label with a dry-erase marker. 6 containers to the fridge, 4 to the freezer. Move freezer containers to the fridge the night before eating.

**Notes:**

UNTESTED — quantities are planned, not observed. Weigh the shredded chicken
after step 5, the cooked rice after step 10, and the broccoli after step 11,
then promote with a real date.

This is built for a week with no time in it. Nothing touches an oven, there
are no sheet pans to wash, and the only knife work is dicing celery at eating
time. The crockpot runs unattended for four to five hours.

**Frozen broccoli is the deliberate choice, not a compromise.** Fresh crowns
would mean washing, trimming, cutting, oiling, two roasting batches and a
drain — 20–25 minutes, which is more than this recipe's entire hands-on
budget. Frozen florets are already blanched, so they only need steaming, and
1000 g of frozen is 1000 g in the containers with no yield loss to plan
around. Roasted tastes better; on a week like this it is not worth the time.

**The sauce is cut to two-thirds and the tang rebuilt with vinegar.** Frank's
RedHot is 190 mg of sodium per teaspoon, so the 1 ½ cups a full-strength
version calls for lands around 1500 mg per container — worse than the bottled
teriyaki, and the worst number this rotation would have. ⅔ cup comes in near
760 mg, under the ceiling.

Cutting hot sauce alone would leave it bland, so the 2 tbsp of white vinegar
restores the acidity and the cayenne restores the heat, neither of which
carries any sodium. Same move as the ground beef sauce: rebuild the flavour
from unsalted components rather than accepting what the bottle gives you.

If you want the full-heat version, use 1 ½ cups Frank's and drop the vinegar
and cayenne. It will be better buffalo and roughly 1500 mg of sodium, and the
ranch adds another ~260 mg on top at eating time.

**Chicken is 5 ¼ lbs, not the 6 ½ an earlier draft suggested.** Chicken breast
runs about 31 g of protein per 100 g against 26 for beef and turkey, so it
needs a smaller portion for the same protein. At 180 g per container this
comes to 62 g of protein, over the 45–55 band; 155 g lands at 54 g. Planned
against the 0.65 crockpot yield in standing parameters, not the 0.76 that
draft assumed — 0.76 is the top of the observed range and the one measurement
this repo has is 0.637.

Ranch goes on at eating time, never packed — it separates when reheated.
Celery diced fresh, not on prep day.
