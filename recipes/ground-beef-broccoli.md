## Ground Beef and Broccoli Bowls

**Serves:** 10
**Prep Time:** 20 min
**Cook Time:** 55 min

**Ingredients:**

  - 5 lbs ground beef (90/10)
  - ~5 ½ lbs broccoli florets (~2500 g, yields ~1200 g roasted)
  - 3 ½ cups jasmine rice (dry)
  - ⅔ cup low-sodium soy sauce
  - ¾ cup low-sodium beef broth
  - 8–9 cloves garlic, minced
  - 2 tbsp fresh ginger, minced (or 2 tsp ground)
  - 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  - 2 tbsp brown sugar
  - 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  - 2 tbsp cornstarch + 4 tbsp cold water
  - ½ tsp red pepper flakes (optional)
  - Olive oil, salt, pepper
  - Scallions and sriracha (added fresh when eating)

**Steps:**

1.  Preheat the main oven to 425°F with the sheet pans inside, and the Ninja toaster oven to 400°F air roast. Hot pans sear on contact instead of steaming.

2.  Rinse 3 ½ cups jasmine rice 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear. Add to the Instant Pot with 3 ½ cups water (1:1).

3.  Confirm the valve is set to SEALING. Pressure Cook (Manual) on High for 3 minutes, natural release 10 minutes, then quick release. Start this first — it takes ~10 minutes to come to pressure. Fluff and leave the lid off to cool.

4.  Cut the broccoli into even florets, roughly 1 ½ inches. Uneven pieces mean the small ones burn before the large ones soften.

5.  Toss the broccoli with olive oil, salt, and pepper. Spread across 2 preheated pans in a single layer, nothing touching.

6.  Roast at 425°F in the main oven for 18–22 minutes, flipping halfway, until the edges char slightly. 5 ½ lbs will not fit on 2 pans in a single layer — air roast the remainder at 400°F in the Ninja toaster oven for 12–15 minutes, running in parallel.

7.  Heat a large pan on the stovetop over medium-high. Add 5 lbs ground beef, breaking it up as it cooks. Brown 12–15 minutes until no pink remains. Work in 2 batches if the pan is crowded — crowding steams the meat instead of browning it.

8.  Drain the fat thoroughly. 90/10 renders less than 80/20 but skipping this still makes the bowls greasy by day 3.

9.  In a saucepan, combine ⅔ cup low-sodium soy sauce, ¾ cup beef broth, the minced garlic and ginger, 2 tbsp brown sugar, 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil, and the red pepper flakes. Simmer 4–5 minutes to cook the raw edge off the garlic.

10. Taste the sauce before thickening. It should read savory and salty with only a background sweetness — if it tastes like a glaze, it has gone the wrong direction.

11. Whisk 2 tbsp cornstarch into 4 tbsp cold water, then stir the slurry into the simmering sauce. Cook 1–2 minutes until it coats the back of a spoon.

12. Remove the sauce from the heat and stir in 2 tbsp rice vinegar. The acid goes in last — simmering it dulls the brightness that keeps this from tasting flat.

13. Pour the sauce over the drained beef and stir to coat. Let it sit 5 minutes so the beef takes it up.

14. Cool the beef, rice, and broccoli 10–15 minutes before portioning. Sealing hot food causes condensation and sogginess.

15. Assembly line across 10 open containers: 170 g beef, then 140 g rice, then 120 g broccoli. No separate sauce container — the sauce is already in the beef.

16. Label with a dry-erase marker. 6 containers to the fridge, 4 to the freezer. Move freezer containers to the fridge the night before eating.

**Notes:**

UNTESTED — quantities are planned, not observed. Weigh the browned beef after
step 8, the cooked rice after step 3, and the roasted broccoli after step 6,
then promote with a real date.

Planning arithmetic, worked against `docs/standing-parameters.md`:

  beef       1700 g cooked / 0.75 = 2267 g raw = 5 lbs
  broccoli   1200 g roasted / 0.48 = 2500 g raw = 5 ½ lbs
  rice       3 ½ cups dry at ~410 g per cup = ~1435 g

Buy 5 ½ lbs of broccoli, not the 3 ½–4 that an older version of standing
parameters implied. Measured yield is 0.48, and the old figure under-buys by
about 40%. It is a lot of volume — plan on the toaster oven taking the
overflow.

The sauce is deliberately savory rather than sweet. 2 tbsp of brown sugar
across 10 containers is about 3 g of added sugar each, against 17 g in the
honey garlic version that was rejected for tasting too sweet. Soy and sesame
carry it, the vinegar keeps it bright, and the sugar is there only to round
the salt. If it still reads flat after step 12, add vinegar before sugar.

Projected sodium is ~750 mg, under the 800 ceiling but not by much, and it is
almost entirely the soy. Use low-sodium soy and low-sodium broth — regular soy
alone would push this past 1100 mg.

Broccoli softens slightly in the freezer but holds better than zucchini.
