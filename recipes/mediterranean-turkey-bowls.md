## Mediterranean Turkey Bowls

**Serves:** 10
**Prep Time:** 20 min
**Cook Time:** 55 min

**Ingredients:**

  - 4 packages ground turkey (93/7), ~4.8 lbs total
  - 2 tbsp dried oregano
  - 5–6 cloves garlic, minced
  - 2 tsp ground cumin
  - 2 tsp paprika
  - 3 tbsp lemon juice (for the turkey)
  - 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice (dry)
  - 3–4 tbsp lemon juice (for the rice)
  - 1 tbsp dried dill or parsley
  - ~3 lbs zucchini (~1360 g, yields 825–975 g roasted)
  - 6 bell peppers (~340–620 g roasted; peppers vary hugely)
  - 3 red onions (~240–295 g roasted)
  - Olive oil, salt, pepper
  - Tzatziki, feta, cherry tomatoes, cucumber (added fresh when eating)

**Steps:**

1.  Preheat the main oven to 425°F with the sheet pans inside. Hot pans sear on contact instead of steaming.

2.  Rinse 3 ¼ cups jasmine rice 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear. Add to the Instant Pot with 3 ¼ cups water (1:1). Measure the water carefully — an over-filled pot gives waterlogged rice that packs soggy.

3.  Confirm the valve is set to SEALING. Pressure Cook (Manual) on High for 3 minutes, natural release 10 minutes, then quick release. Start this first — it takes ~10 minutes to come to pressure.

4.  Cut the zucchini into thick ½-inch half-moons. Thin slices are mush by day 3.

5.  Chop the 6 bell peppers and 3 red onions into pieces the same size as the zucchini so they roast evenly.

6.  Toss the vegetables with olive oil, salt, and pepper. Spread across 2 preheated pans in a single layer, nothing touching.

7.  Roast the vegetables at 425°F in the main oven for 20–25 minutes, flipping halfway. The edges should caramelize.

8.  Heat a large pan on the stovetop over medium-high. Add ~4.8 lbs ground turkey, breaking it up as it cooks. Brown 10–12 minutes until no pink remains and it reaches 165°F. Work in 2 batches if the pan is crowded — crowding steams the meat instead of browning it.

9.  Drain the excess liquid, then add the minced garlic, 2 tbsp oregano, 2 tsp cumin, 2 tsp paprika, salt, and pepper. Cook 2–3 minutes until fragrant.

10. Remove the turkey from the heat and stir in 3 tbsp lemon juice. The acid at the end is what makes it taste Mediterranean rather than generic.

11. Fluff the rice, then stir in 3–4 tbsp lemon juice, 1 tbsp dried dill or parsley, and salt. Add the lemon after cooking, not before.

12. Let the turkey, rice, and vegetables cool 10–15 minutes before portioning. Sealing hot food causes condensation and sogginess.

13. Assembly line across 10 open containers: 155 g turkey, then 148 g rice, then 150 g roasted vegetables. No separate sauce container — the seasoning is already in the meat and the rice.

14. Label with a dry-erase marker. 6 containers to the fridge, 4 to the freezer. Move freezer containers to the fridge the night before eating.

**Notes:**

Two sessions behind these numbers, and they disagree more than anything else
in the rotation:

              undated log        2026-08-31
  turkey      4.6 lb → 1500 g    4.8 lb → 1780 g   (0.719 vs 0.818)
  zucchini    2.31 lb → 750 g    3.4 lb → 935 g    (0.716 vs 0.606)
  peppers     3 → 170 g          6 → 620 g         (57 vs 103 g each)
  onions      3 → 240 g          3 → 295 g         (80 vs 98 g each)

**Peppers are the reason this recipe now calls for 6.** A pepper is not a unit
of measurement — the two sessions differ by nearly 2× per pepper, a wider
spread than broccoli crowns. Six covers the small end without overwhelming the
batch at the large end. Aldi does not let you weigh produce in store, so the
weight range is here to plan against rather than to shop by.

**Turkey at 0.818 is a light-drain number.** The prior session drained harder
and got 0.719 on the same product. Plan at 0.72 and treat anything above as
surplus; planning high would leave you short. Watch whether the lighter drain
makes these containers greasier by day 4 — that is the tradeoff the taco bowls
notes warn about.

The 2026-08-31 rice is not recorded. 3 ½ cups came out at 1840 g, or 526 g per
dry cup against 407–457 across three prior sessions — too much water in the
pot, not more rice. A measurement known to be contaminated is worse than none,
because it looks exactly like signal, and averaging it in would have raised
the planning figure and under-bought rice on every recipe after it.

Peppers hold up better than zucchini in the freezer. If you are freezing half
the batch, go lighter on zucchini and heavier on peppers in the freezer
containers.

This is the no-chicken option in the rotation, and the only one with no
bottled sauce anywhere in it — which is why the sodium projection is the
lowest of the four.

Cold toppings — tzatziki, feta, cherry tomatoes, cucumber — go on fresh at
eating time, never packed. Aldi carries tzatziki and feta in the refrigerated
section.

Reheat, then top. A dollop of tzatziki and a handful of halved tomatoes takes
thirty seconds and is the difference between a reheated bowl and something
that tastes made that day.
